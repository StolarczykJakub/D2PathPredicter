## What early imagery can identify

Let `X` be the complete allowed observation at a checkpoint, including its reveal mask and the current player location. Let `Y` be the true sector of an eligible target. The optimal direction on observations with the same `X` has conditional accuracy `max_y P(Y = y | X)`. Overall top-1 accuracy at full coverage is the expectation of that quantity. For selective prediction, that expectation is taken over the selected fraction and depends on how well the method can identify low-uncertainty cases. More compute cannot resolve disagreements inside an observation-equivalence class. A finite real sample usually gives sparse or singleton exact keys, so its in-sample majority accuracy can substantially overestimate the limit for unseen layouts.

An audit-only Python calculation checked two synthetic completions with the same observed three horizontal rooms centred at `(-1,0), (0,0), (1,0)` and the player at `(0,0)`. The first completion placed NEXT at `(1,2)` and WP at `(3,0)`. The other placed NEXT at `(1,-2)` and WP at `(1,-3)`. The branches exit the observed corridor beyond the visible mask. Each target splits 50/50 under equal occurrence. Keeping only the first completion in a closed reference pool gives a unique zero-mismatch fit with both sectors wrong on the second completion. A one-unit player shift from `(0,0)` to `(1,0)` also flips the sector to `(5,5)` from `+X` to `+Y` under the protocol. These examples verify logical failure modes. Their frequency in either real game is unknown. [F-05](FINDINGS.md#f-05) · [F-08](FINDINGS.md#f-08)

Early observations can contain mostly entrance cues. If the entire retained feature is the two-way entrance axis, it carries at most one bit. With any finite observed feature vocabulary, more reachable seeds than distinct prefixes force prefix collisions. The same complete room arrangement can in principle arise through different generation histories or seeds. Neither actual complete-layout collision rates nor distinct-prefix counts for D2R were established. Even a perfect extractor cannot read a hidden branch, seed/state, destination identity without a visible cue, missing layer, or a unique absolute world position from a cropped relative image. Same geometry can also conceal different target labels. The computational problem of generator search and the statistical ambiguity of prediction both matter.

Additional reliable observations can refine equivalence classes. For a fixed cohort and nested observations the average best attainable accuracy cannot decrease, but an individual posterior can become less certain and a practical method can degrade under noise, lost registration or sparse lookup. Discovery also changes the sample. A useful result must show that NEXT or WP becomes predictable while still hidden, and that the geometry adds value on full layouts absent from training. At arrival the orientation prior is the evidence-led comparator. Improvement by three rooms and waypoint/exit predictability remain empirical questions. ## Algorithm audit

`M` is the number of training maps, `A` plausible alignments, `K` observed features, `F` stored features per map, `P` distinct local keys and `R` indexed occurrences. Costs assume a fixed coordinate frame or a bounded alignment search. There are no measured accuracy or speed values for these methods in the project.

| Approach | Input, preprocessing, runtime and memory | Accuracy limits and disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Template matching | Compare visible oriented lines or room signatures against masked projected templates. Normalize origin/scale and enumerate allowed alignments. Geometry scan `O(MAK)` with indexed feature access and `O(MF)` storage, or scan a smaller dictionary of local templates. Raster variants add pixel/window costs. | Repeated or symmetric pieces and missing or noisy lines produce ties. Can recognize unseen combinations of known templates, but target inference still needs empirical conditional evidence. Use manual geometry first and keep raster matching deferred. |
| Geometric hashing | Index quantized features relative to ordered anchor pairs or other small bases. All anchor pairs with all remaining features cost up to `O(MF³)` work and votes if fully materialized. Query cost follows tested bases and posting-list lengths, with a verification pass. | Can tolerate missing lines when an anchor survives. False anchors, quantization, repetitive corridors and scale errors can flood buckets or miss valid matches. Helps indexing and alignment only. Defer unless a measured bottleneck justifies a bounded key design. |
| Graph matching | Use an entrance-rooted embedded graph of visible nodes/links with known-absent vs unknown boundaries. With indexed cells the bounded comparison is `O(MAK)`. General unanchored subgraph isomorphism can require exponential search. Store linear node/edge records. | An anchored prefix is interpretable and can recur on unseen maps. False or hidden edges, disconnected fragments and symmetric graphs can invalidate a hard match. Test a tiny feature key before a full reference scan and skip general graph search. |
| Map fingerprinting | Hash canonical local feature keys and retain counts or postings. Construction roughly `O(RK)` for keys of size `K`, expected constant bucket access after key calculation, followed by verification. Histograms `O(P)`, postings `O(R)`. | A full-map hash handles known-map identity only. Partial keys have intentional collisions and sparse training support. Noise can create false misses. Favor smoothed local keys with prior backoff if pilot results show measurable support. |
| Rule-based prediction | Use a small empirical direction prior from visible entrance orientation and player location, plus only independently validated area rules. Training is linear in examples. Fixed bins cost constant time and small storage, while a raw target-offset sample scan scales with development cases. | Cheapest comparator. Broad and local blind spots are expected. Hard-coding a global bearing from a special room's orientation is unjustified. Rules transfer only within validated conditions. |
| Seed reconstruction | Needs a compatible generator, seed distribution and discriminative observables. Offline exhaustive costs scale with the seed domain and generation cost. Online cost is a scan or lookup plus candidate generation and validation. | Determinism does not imply uniqueness. Early masks and noisy features leave multiple seeds. More candidates can agree or conflict in target space. The full Normal Tower procedure uses far richer observations. Keep out of Phase 1. |
| Hybrid prediction | Add a few visible features to the entrance prior with support-based smoothing, calibrated abstention and a prior fallback. One compact key or bounded table per target can be sufficient. Whole-map aggregation, if tested, costs the scan plus candidate alignment and voting. | Best minimal candidate to test for incremental value. Compare under identical, hidden-target, held-out checkpoints. A vote share from sampled references is sensitive to selection and weighting. No accuracy advantage is established. |

## Search space and cost sensitivity

Illustrative sizing only. Packed-map payload assumes 40 feature records × 16 bytes per map (640 bytes), before labels, offsets, index, allocation, parsing or working set. The 20-byte column models a compact seed record as in the Tower deployment estimate. For time, assume 0.10–1.0 ms per generated item on one worker and exclude extraction, writes, sorting and retries. The last column assumes two alignments, 24 compared features each and a sustained 20 million feature checks/s under favorable locality. A check includes a single feature access and comparison. The rate is hypothetical. Time may drop with early rejection and grow with memory traffic, noisy verification or all candidates surviving. For illustrative full-domain rows, each seed incurs one record/trial before deduplication. D2R full-domain size is unknown. [Tower sizing](https://github.com/emmericp/diablo2-maps/blob/main/Dockerfile#L18-L20)

| Items | Packed geometry | 20-byte records | Generation on one worker | Full scan per update |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 10,000 | 6.4 MB | 0.20 MB | 1–10 s | 24 ms |
| 100,000 | 64 MB | 2.0 MB | 10–100 s | 240 ms |
| 1,000,000 | 640 MB | 20 MB | 1.7–16.7 min | 2.4 s |
| 10,000,000 | 6.4 GB | 200 MB | 16.7 min–2.78 h | 24 s |
| `2³¹` | 1.37 TB | 42.95 GB / 40 GiB | 2.49–24.86 d | 85.9 min |
| `2³²` | 2.75 TB | 85.90 GB / 80 GiB | 4.97–49.71 d | 171.8 min |

Under these assumptions, 100,000 maps already exceed the warm-update budget in a full scan and leave 36 MB of the proposed working-set budget for the process and auxiliary data. Disk and memory-mapping may lower residency and add IO or page faults. Keep seed count, distinct layout count, distinct geometry plus labels, prefix collision rate and occurrence frequencies as separate measurements. Under independent draws and a uniform domain over `U` distinct configurations, a particular configuration's chance to occur somewhere in `M` draws is `1 - (1 - 1/U)^M`. Neither `U` nor uniformity is established. Wider reference coverage cannot fix observation ambiguity and may expose more legitimate conflicting outcomes. Exact and noisy keys can create one huge bucket or many empty buckets, respectively. Report p95 and worst bucket/latency in any index test.

A fixed-checkpoint/player-bin local histogram can store four 32-bit counts per target plus an illustrative 16-byte key, or 48 bytes per bucket before other metadata. At 1,000 or 100,000 keys that is 48 kB or 4.8 MB of payload. This only preserves predictive value when the retained features and position representation are sufficiently informative. Moving players need position bins, or target-offset summaries followed by re-binning. Low-count and out-of-domain patterns need backoff or abstention. A size sweep should compare accuracy, coverage, NO MATCH rate and memory for the compact table and scan on common splits before choosing storage. [F-11](FINDINGS.md#f-11)
